# CEX Control Boundary v1

## Core boundary

- Login is not operational control.
- Account-holder recovery is not third-party takeover.
- Resetting email, phone, Authenticator or identity access may leave withdrawal, P2P, transfer, address-book or security restrictions.
- Withdrawal may require renewed KYC/identity checks, manual review, whitelist/address controls, device checks or a cooling period.
- Technical possession of credentials is not legal authorization, ownership or permission to bypass platform terms.
- While capable, the account holder's own lawful transfer to a recipient-controlled account/wallet is the preferred handoff path.
- Death, incapacity or inability to authorize requires the platform's official succession process and, where applicable, qualified legal review.

## Four separate paths

1. `ACCOUNT_HOLDER_RECOVERY`: the verified holder follows official recovery and separately confirms withdrawal capability.
2. `OWNER_DIRECTED_TRANSFER`: the capable holder initiates a normal lawful withdrawal after destination/network verification and applicable restrictions.
3. `THIRD_PARTY_ACCESS`: credentials may expose a login screen or account view, but control is not assured and must never be represented as authorized takeover.
4. `OFFICIAL_SUCCESSION`: a verified legal representative submits official documents; platform/compliance determines the outcome.

## Mandatory stop/escalation

Stop and use official support when identity differs, account is frozen, facial/KYC review fails, the requested transfer is unfamiliar, a cooling period is active, or anyone requests passwords, OTPs, authenticator seeds, remote control or an off-platform payment. Ownership, incapacity and estate questions require human/legal escalation.

## Product rule

CJAS may describe a platform-specific fact only from an APPROVED, unexpired Claim with matching region/product scope. Generic CEX guidance must remain visibly labeled `CJAS_GUIDANCE`. No CEX may be labeled “arbitrary third party can fully control.”
